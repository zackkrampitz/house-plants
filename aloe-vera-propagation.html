Propagation

The practical method for this plant, with timing and aftercare.

Best method

Separate basal pups with their own roots

Timing & what you need

Aim for active spring or summer growth. Indoors, warmth and adequate light matter more than the month. Use a healthy parent plant, clean sharp tools, a small draining pot and the medium described below. Avoid propagating a plant already stressed by pests or root rot.

Step by step

  1. In spring or summer, select a pup with several leaves, preferably at least one-quarter to one-third the mother’s size and with roots of its own.
  2. Unpot the plant and expose the connection. Ease the pup away; use a clean blade if necessary, keeping its roots.
  3. Allow any cut surfaces to seal in airy shade for a few days.
  4. Pot in a small draining container of airy succulent mix. After the wound seals, water cautiously and allow drying between waterings.

Aftercare & fertilizer

Keep warm in bright indirect light initially, then gradually restore stronger light. Do not keep rooting pups continually wet or fertilize before they establish.

How long it takes

A rooted pup may settle over 3–6 weeks. Unrooted pups take longer; new leaves may take several months.

Plant-specific notes

Aloe leaf pieces usually rot rather than form a new plant. Rooted pups are the practical method. A plant leaning toward light needs more light introduced gradually, not a moss pole.

Times are approximate. Light, warmth, season and plant condition can change them considerably. A healthy new root system matters more than a fixed number of days.
