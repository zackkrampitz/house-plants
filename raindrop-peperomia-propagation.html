Propagation

The practical method for this plant, with timing and aftercare.

Best method

Stem-tip cuttings with a node

Timing & what you need

Aim for active spring or summer growth. Indoors, warmth and adequate light matter more than the month. Use a healthy parent plant, clean sharp tools, a small draining pot and the medium described below. Avoid propagating a plant already stressed by pests or root rot.

Step by step

  1. In spring or summer, choose a healthy tip with 2–3 leaves and a stem node. Cut below that node with clean scissors.
  2. Remove the lowest leaf so its node can go into the rooting medium.
  3. Insert the node into lightly moist potting mix/perlite, or place it in water with leaves above the waterline.
  4. Transfer water-rooted cuttings to a small draining pot once roots are about 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm) long and branching.

Aftercare & fertilizer

Keep warm in bright indirect light. Maintain gentle moisture while rooting; avoid saturation around the fleshy stem. Gradually allow more drying once established. Do not feed before rooting.

How long it takes

Roots may form in 3–8 weeks; new growth and a fuller plant take longer.

Plant-specific notes

Leaf-and-petiole cuttings can also work but are often slower. Stem cuttings are a practical way to keep the mother compact and later plant several rooted cuttings together.

Times are approximate. Light, warmth, season and plant condition can change them considerably. A healthy new root system matters more than a fixed number of days.
