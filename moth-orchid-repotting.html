Repotting

Choose the right container and mix, then help the roots settle.

When to repot

Best time: spring to early summer, while actively growing. Repot sooner if there is root rot, broken drainage or a failing pot. Check the root ball first; refresh tired mix in the same pot if the roots do not need more room. Slow growth alone is not proof it needs upsizing.

Pot material, shape & size

A clear plastic orchid pot with ample holes or slots is ideal for seeing roots and moisture. A slotted ceramic orchid pot also works but can dry faster. Use normal depth and just enough width for the healthy roots—often the same size, or one size larger. Avoid a large sealed glass vessel.

Your soils & extra materials

You have PRO-MIX Premium Organic Vegetable & Herb Mix, PRO-MIX Premium Cactus Mix, perlite and orchid mix. The orchid mix’s ingredients are not confirmed: check whether it is a coarse bark-based epiphytic orchid mix or a finer soil-like product before using it in the orchid and bromeliad recipes.

Soil / growing medium

Use your orchid mix if it is a coarse, bark-based mix labelled for Phalaenopsis or epiphytic orchids. Use it as supplied if already well aerated; no PRO-MIX soil is needed. If it is mainly fine peat or soil-like material, it is not a suitable replacement for bark: obtain a coarse Phalaenopsis bark mix instead. Moisten or pre-soak dry bark according to the bag directions, then drain. Perlite is optional if the orchid mix already drains well.

Rocks & drainage

Do not add a gravel, stone or broken-pot layer at the bottom. It takes space from roots and can leave the soil above wetter. Use drainage holes instead; a small piece of mesh can retain mix without blocking them. Perlite or pumice, where specified, goes throughout the mix. Decorative stones are optional but can hide soil moisture; leaving the surface open is easier for checking.

Fertilizer at repotting

No special transplant fertilizer is needed. After trimming roots, allow wounds to heal; resume weak orchid fertilizer after new root growth, commonly a few weeks later. Fresh bark is not a reason to use strong fertilizer. Follow the product label and flush salts occasionally.

How to repot & aftercare

Repot after flowering when new roots start, unless rot requires immediate action. Soften roots with water, remove decomposed bark gently, and trim only hollow, mushy or dead roots with clean tools. Healthy roots may be green, silver or pale. Keep the crown above the medium; do not force all aerial roots into the pot. Water the new bark and drain thoroughly, keeping the crown dry.

Before you begin

Use a clean pot and tools. Pre-mix ingredients and check that drainage works. Do not fertilize as a treatment for stressed roots. After repotting, check moisture directly: the new mix can dry at a different speed from the old root ball.

Mix proportions are practical starting recipes by volume, not a fixed requirement. Adjust to the base mix, indoor light and drying rate. Pick a commercial mix with ingredients that fit the plant; the label matters more than the product name.
