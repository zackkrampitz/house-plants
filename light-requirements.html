Light Requirements

Ideal light and practical window placement for all 20 plants. These are starting placements for an Ottawa home; trees, balconies, tinted glass and distance from the window can change how much light reaches a leaf.

Sunny

Reserve the brightest spots for citrus, agave, Peruvian apple cactus, ponytail palm and Cape sundew. Introduce stronger sun gradually.

Bright & filtered

Most of your tropical plants want abundant light with protection from hot midday or afternoon beams. East windows or sheer curtains often work.

Gentler indirect light

Calathea prefers protected light. Both ZZ plants grow best with moderate brightness, even though they tolerate darker positions.

Direct vs. indirect: direct sun means a sunbeam falls on the leaves. Bright indirect light is a bright position without a strong beam. Window direction alone does not guarantee enough light. Check the actual plant position across the day.

Winter and grow lights

Prioritize supplemental light for citrus, the cactus, agave and sundew when winter window light is inadequate. A practical starting timer is 12–14 hours per day for foliage plants, and 14–16 hours for flowering or fruiting plants under artificial light. Count daylight and lamp exposure together and preserve a nightly dark period. Hours alone cannot compensate for a weak lamp.

Use a white full-spectrum grow light. Set distance using its manufacturer’s coverage and intensity guidance at leaf height, then watch new growth. One fixed distance will not suit every lamp. Tall plants can shade smaller pots below them.

Read the plant’s response

  • Too little light: elongated stems, small new leaves, leaning, fewer flowers or thinning cactus growth.
  • Too much light: bleached patches, crisp scorched areas or overheating. Move into filtered light and increase exposure gradually over 1–2 weeks.
  • Reassess watering after moving a plant: brighter light usually increases water use; a darker location often reduces it.
