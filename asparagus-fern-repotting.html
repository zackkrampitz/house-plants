Repotting

Choose the right container and mix, then help the roots settle.

When to repot

Best time: spring to early summer, while actively growing. Repot sooner if there is root rot, broken drainage or a failing pot. Check the root ball first; refresh tired mix in the same pot if the roots do not need more room. Slow growth alone is not proof it needs upsizing.

Pot material, shape & size

Plastic or glazed ceramic with drainage holes helps retain the moisture this plant likes. Use a normal-depth, broad, stable pot; its thick storage roots need room. Increase diameter by only 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm).

Soil / growing medium

Suggested starting mix by volume: 3 parts indoor potting mix + 1 part perlite or pumice. The mix should hold some moisture while draining freely. Do not use dense garden soil.

Rocks & drainage

Do not add a gravel, stone or broken-pot layer at the bottom. It takes space from roots and can leave the soil above wetter. Use drainage holes instead; a small piece of mesh can retain mix without blocking them. Perlite or pumice, where specified, goes throughout the mix. Decorative stones are optional but can hide soil moisture; leaving the surface open is easier for checking.

Fertilizer at repotting

No special transplant or “root booster” fertilizer is needed. Do not fertilize bare or injured roots. If the fresh mix contains fertilizer, follow its feeding interval and avoid doubling up. For an uncharged mix, wait roughly 3–4 weeks and until growth resumes, then restart the plant’s normal diluted fertilizer. In slow winter growth, wait longer.

How to repot & aftercare

Gently loosen the outer root mat. Preserve as many fleshy storage roots as possible. Plant at the original depth; do not bury stem bases. Water thoroughly after potting, drain, and keep in bright indirect light while it settles.

Before you begin

Use a clean pot and tools. Pre-mix ingredients and check that drainage works. Do not fertilize as a treatment for stressed roots. After repotting, check moisture directly: the new mix can dry at a different speed from the old root ball.

Mix proportions are practical starting recipes by volume, not a fixed requirement. Adjust to the base mix, indoor light and drying rate. Pick a commercial mix with ingredients that fit the plant; the label matters more than the product name.
