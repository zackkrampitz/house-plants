Fertilizer Schedule

One feeding reference for all 20 plants in your collection. These are starting intervals for established plants in potting mix, based on your existing care guides. New growth and the product label determine when to feed.

March–May · restart gently

Resume when new leaves or roots appear and light improves. Use a weak first feeding; avoid feeding a stressed plant.

June–August · active growth

Use the plant intervals below. Feed at a normal watering when the mix needs water, never just to meet a date.

September–February · reassess

Ottawa’s shorter days often slow growth. Reduce in fall and pause most plants in winter unless strong light supports new growth.

Easy routine: check monthly feeders every 4 weeks, begonias / Swiss cheese vine / orchid / citrus every 2–4 weeks, and ZZ plants / succulents every 6–8 weeks during active growth. These are check-in windows, not automatic feeding dates. Cape sundew gets no ordinary soil fertilizer.

Fertilizer by plant

“Half strength” means half the label’s normal concentration, not twice as much water applied to the pot. Example: a standard 4 mL/L rate becomes 2 mL/L. If the label already specifies a weak frequent-feeding rate, follow it rather than automatically halving it again.

Before each feeding

  • Confirm active growth, adequate light and healthy roots. Fertilizer cannot fix insufficient light or root rot.
  • Do not feed a bone-dry root ball. Moisten it first and let excess drain.
  • Check for fertilizer already in the mix. Do not stack liquid feeding on top of slow-release nutrients without label guidance.
  • After substantial root disturbance, wait for recovery and renewed growth. Repotting does not require a special fertilizer.
  • Liquid and granular products use different schedules. The intervals above are for liquid feeding; follow granular or slow-release labels instead.
