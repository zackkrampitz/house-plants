Repotting

Choose the right container and mix, then help the roots settle.

When to repot

Best time: spring to early summer, while actively growing. Repot sooner if there is root rot, broken drainage or a failing pot. Check the root ball first; refresh tired mix in the same pot if the roots do not need more room. Slow growth alone is not proof it needs upsizing.

Pot material, shape & size

Plastic is the easiest choice; glazed ceramic can work if it has drainage and no exposed porous clay. Avoid unglazed terracotta, which can release minerals. Use a normal-to-deep pot, often about 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) deep for a mature plant, sized to the actual roots and crowns.

Your soils & extra materials

You have PRO-MIX Premium Organic Vegetable & Herb Mix, PRO-MIX Premium Cactus Mix, perlite and orchid mix. The orchid mix’s ingredients are not confirmed: check whether it is a coarse bark-based epiphytic orchid mix or a finer soil-like product before using it in the orchid and bromeliad recipes.

Soil / growing medium

Do not use either PRO-MIX soil or your orchid mix for this plant. Additional material needed unless already obtained separately: plain, unfertilized sphagnum peat moss, or a ready-made fertilizer-free sundew/carnivorous-plant mix. If your perlite is plain and fertilizer-free, mix 1 part of it with 1 part plain sphagnum peat by volume. If the perlite has added fertilizer, obtain plain perlite first. Pre-moisten with distilled, RO or rainwater. Check ingredients for added fertilizer and lime; use neither.

Rocks & drainage

No bottom layer of stones, gravel or pottery shards. Use an open drainage hole so tray water reaches the medium. Only low-mineral materials belong in the mix; avoid limestone, unknown gravel and enriched perlite. Keep the surface accessible for moisture checks.

Fertilizer at repotting

No fertilizer in the medium, no rooting hormone and no fertilizer “starter” charge. Nutrients come from prey on the leaves; keep normal fertilizer out of the soil and tray water.

How to repot & aftercare

Keep roots wet during the job. Remove degraded or contaminated medium gently, position the crown at the surface, and keep the growth point above the mix. Return the pot to a shallow tray of distilled, RO or clean rainwater. Repot during active growth; this species does not need winter dormancy.

Before you begin

Use a clean pot and tools. Pre-mix ingredients and check that drainage works. Do not fertilize as a treatment for stressed roots. After repotting, check moisture directly: the new mix can dry at a different speed from the old root ball.

Mix proportions are practical starting recipes by volume, not a fixed requirement. Adjust to the base mix, indoor light and drying rate. Pick a commercial mix with ingredients that fit the plant; the label matters more than the product name.
