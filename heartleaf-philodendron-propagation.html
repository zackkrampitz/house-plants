Propagation

The practical method for this plant, with timing and aftercare.

Best method

Stem-node cuttings

Timing & what you need

Aim for active spring or summer growth. Indoors, warmth and adequate light matter more than the month. Use a healthy parent plant, clean sharp tools, a small draining pot and the medium described below. Avoid propagating a plant already stressed by pests or root rot.

Step by step

  1. Take cuttings during active spring or summer growth. Locate a node: the joint where a leaf and often an aerial root emerge from the stem.
  2. Cut just below the node with clean scissors. A tip with 1–3 nodes and a few leaves is easy to handle.
  3. Remove the lowest leaf if needed. Put a node into clean water or lightly moist propagation mix; keep leaves above it. Change water about weekly.
  4. Pot into a small draining container when roots are about 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm) long and beginning to branch. Plant several established cuttings together for a fuller pot.

Aftercare & fertilizer

Keep warm in bright indirect light. Water-rooted cuttings need lightly moist mix during the first week or two after transfer; then gradually return to the normal watering trigger. Skip fertilizer until rooted and showing growth.

How long it takes

Roots often develop in 2–6 weeks; allow longer to establish a substantial new vine.

Plant-specific notes

A leaf with its leaf stalk but no stem node can stay alive or form roots without making a new vine. Always include a node. Cut the mother just above a node if you want it to branch.

Times are approximate. Light, warmth, season and plant condition can change them considerably. A healthy new root system matters more than a fixed number of days.
