House Plants / Repotting

Sago Palm

Keep away from dogs and cats

All parts of sago palm are highly toxic if eaten, including seeds and pups. Keep the whole plant and removed material inaccessible to your dog. Suspected ingestion needs immediate veterinary advice.

Repotting

Choose the right container and mix, then help the roots settle.

When to repot

Best time: spring to early summer, while actively growing. Repot sooner if there is root rot, broken drainage or a failing pot. Check the root ball first; refresh tired mix in the same pot if the roots do not need more room. Slow growth alone is not proof it needs upsizing.

Pot material, shape & size

A heavy terracotta pot with drainage is useful; plastic can work if the mix drains well. Choose normal-to-deep proportions to fit the roots, just 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm) wider than the root ball. The very large terracotta pot in your photo would add too much wet mix if planted directly into it now.

Your soils & extra materials

You have PRO-MIX Premium Organic Vegetable & Herb Mix, PRO-MIX Premium Cactus Mix, perlite and orchid mix. The orchid mix’s ingredients are not confirmed: check whether it is a coarse bark-based epiphytic orchid mix or a finer soil-like product before using it in the orchid and bromeliad recipes.

Soil / growing medium

Use your PRO-MIX Premium Cactus Mix as the base. Suggested starting recipe by volume: 2 parts PRO-MIX Premium Cactus Mix + 1 part plain perlite. Use the perlite you now have for this recipe. Orchid bark is not required for this recipe. Use a snug draining pot and confirm the deeper mix is drying appropriately before watering again.

Rocks & drainage

Do not add a gravel, stone or broken-pot layer at the bottom. It takes space from roots and can leave the soil above wetter. Use drainage holes instead; a small piece of mesh can retain mix without blocking them. Perlite or pumice, where specified, goes throughout the mix. Decorative stones are optional but can hide soil moisture; leaving the surface open is easier for checking.

Fertilizer at repotting

Do not add special transplant or root-booster fertilizer. Your Vegetable & Herb Mix already contains fertilizer; check the Cactus Mix bag for its starter feed too. Do not stack fresh-mix fertilizer with a full liquid feed. Follow the bag’s feeding interval and wait until the plant has recovered and is actively growing before restarting its normal feed. In low-light winter conditions, delay feeding further. Do not fertilize injured or rotting roots.

How to repot & aftercare

Work during spring or early summer growth and disturb the root ball minimally. Keep the swollen trunk at its existing soil level and its crown exposed. If roots remain intact, water and drain to settle; if substantial cuts were needed, let wounds dry before watering. Do not remove healthy fronds or cut the growing point.

Before you begin

Use a clean pot and tools. Pre-mix ingredients and check that drainage works. Do not fertilize as a treatment for stressed roots. After repotting, check moisture directly: the new mix can dry at a different speed from the old root ball.

Mix proportions are practical starting recipes by volume, not a fixed requirement. Adjust to the base mix, indoor light and drying rate. Pick a commercial mix with ingredients that fit the plant; the label matters more than the product name.
