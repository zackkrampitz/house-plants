Propagation

The practical method for this plant, with timing and aftercare.

Best method

Rooted offsets, also called pups

Timing & what you need

Aim for active spring or summer growth. Indoors, warmth and adequate light matter more than the month. Use a healthy parent plant, clean sharp tools, a small draining pot and the medium described below. Avoid propagating a plant already stressed by pests or root rot.

Step by step

  1. In spring or summer, wait for a pup large enough to handle and preferably with roots of its own.
  2. Unpot the plant or uncover the pup’s connection. Separate with a clean knife while preserving its roots.
  3. Let any cut connection dry and seal in airy shade for a few days before potting.
  4. Pot at its original depth in a small container of mineral-rich succulent mix. Keep the cut base from staying wet.

Aftercare & fertilizer

Begin with bright indirect light, then gradually return it to its usual sun exposure. After wounds seal, water sparingly when the mix has dried; do not keep it continually moist.

How long it takes

A rooted pup may settle in a few weeks; an unrooted offset can take many weeks or months.

Plant-specific notes

Not every agave produces pups. Leaf cuttings are not a useful method; seed is the alternative for plants without offsets.

Times are approximate. Light, warmth, season and plant condition can change them considerably. A healthy new root system matters more than a fixed number of days.
