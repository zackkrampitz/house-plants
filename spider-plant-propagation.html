Propagation

The practical method for this plant, with timing and aftercare.

Best method

Root the baby spiderettes

Timing & what you need

Aim for active spring or summer growth. Indoors, warmth and adequate light matter more than the month. Use a healthy parent plant, clean sharp tools, a small draining pot and the medium described below. Avoid propagating a plant already stressed by pests or root rot.

Step by step

  1. During active spring or summer growth, choose a baby with several leaves and visible root nubs.
  2. For the easiest route, leave it attached and set its base on lightly moist mix in a small pot beside the mother; pin the base gently in place.
  3. Keep the small pot lightly moist until the baby has rooted firmly and shows growth. Then cut the connecting runner.
  4. Alternatively, detach a mature baby and root its base in water or potting mix. Keep its central crown above the water or soil surface.

Aftercare & fertilizer

Bright indirect light and warmth help. Keep the rooting pot lightly moist at first, then gradually use the usual surface-dry watering trigger. Feed only once established.

How long it takes

Many babies root in 1–3 weeks; allow roughly 3–6 weeks to establish.

Plant-specific notes

To make a fuller pot, plant several rooted babies together in a modest-size container. The main plant can also be divided if its rooted crowns are crowded.

Times are approximate. Light, warmth, season and plant condition can change them considerably. A healthy new root system matters more than a fixed number of days.
